# Project description (draft)

## The problem

NASA publishes decades of Earth-system data, but for most people it is hard to go
from a data service to a trustworthy answer to simple questions: is it getting
warmer where I live, by how much, and is that a real trend or just noise?

## What EarthScope does

EarthScope is a Streamlit web app that addresses the four questions in the
challenge:

- **What is changing?** The user selects 2-meter air temperature (`T2M`, °C) or 2-meter relative humidity (`RH2M`, %).
- **Where?** EarthScope focuses on the Caribbean. The user selects one of four preset Caribbean locations (Bridgetown, Kingston, Georgetown, Port of Spain), a reference point outside the region (London), or any latitude and longitude, and may add a second place for comparison.
- **How much?** The app reports the linear trend per decade with a 95% confidence interval.
- **Is it significant?** The app reports the p-value and R², and states in plain language whether the trend is statistically significant at the 5% level.

It also provides an interactive chart of annual means with the fitted trend line,
a comparison table, CSV downloads of the annual data and the trend summary, and
panels covering data provenance, the method, and limitations.

## How it works

1. The app requests monthly data for the chosen point and years from the NASA POWER Monthly API.
2. It checks the response and removes NASA's fill value (−999) and any invalid records. Nothing is filled in or interpolated.
3. It averages the 12 monthly values into an annual mean, only for years that have all 12 months, and lists any year it had to exclude.
4. It fits an ordinary least-squares line with SciPy and computes the slope, 95% confidence interval, p-value and R². At least 10 complete years are required.
5. It writes the conclusion from the computed numbers.

## NASA data used

- **NASA POWER Monthly and Annual API** (NASA Langley Research Center POWER Project): <https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/monthly/>
- The meteorological parameters come from **MERRA-2**, the reanalysis produced by NASA's Global Modeling and Assimilation Office.
- Parameters: `T2M` and `RH2M`, monthly, 1981 to the last complete year.

## Example finding

From NASA POWER data retrieved on 14 November 2026, for 1991–2025 (35 complete
years at each location):

- **Bridgetown, Barbados:** annual mean 2-meter air temperature rose by an estimated 0.315 °C per decade (95% CI 0.215 to 0.415; p < 0.001; R² = 0.55).
- **Kingston, Jamaica:** an estimated 0.220 °C per decade (95% CI 0.134 to 0.305; p < 0.001; R² = 0.45).

Both trends are statistically significant at the 5% level under the linear
model's assumptions. Each location was fitted separately; EarthScope does not
test whether the two slopes differ, so no conclusion is drawn about which
location is warming faster.

These are estimates for individual sampled grid cells. They are not national or
Caribbean-wide averages. The other presets differ: for the same period the Port
of Spain cell shows +0.090 °C per decade (95% CI −0.015 to +0.195; p = 0.091),
which is not statistically significant.

## Limitations

- The data are model-derived reanalysis values for a grid cell roughly 50 km across, not thermometer readings at the chosen point.
- A straight line can miss non-linear change. Years are not fully independent (residual lag-1 autocorrelation was about 0.37 for these series), so the intervals and p-values are somewhat optimistic.
- The chosen years affect the slope. Two points do not represent two countries, and the app has no regional or global benchmark, so it cannot say whether the Caribbean is warming faster than the world.
- A trend does not identify a cause, and the app makes no forecasts.
